What the evidence labels mean

Each label describes the argument being made. It is not an automatic true-or-false verdict on the whole proposal. “Legal obstacle” identifies a compatibility question; it does not claim a court has found the policy unlawful.

Internal contradiction
Two claims in the plan cannot both hold as stated.
Legal obstacle
A potential conflict with legal or treaty obligations needs to be resolved.
Costing uncertainty
Assumptions, behaviour or delivery risks prevent confidence in the claimed result.
Strong evidence
Direct evidence supports the specific counterargument, such as the existing legal baseline.
Distributional trade-off
The policy can produce benefits while shifting costs or risks to others.
Reasonable policy with caveats
The objective or mechanism has merit, subject to design and implementation limits.

How this examination works

Each analysis examines the proposal on its own merits. The tests are practical: does the claimed mechanism work, who gains or loses, what does the evidence establish, and what remains uncertain?

Proposals and printed page references were checked against Reform UK’s official 36-page plan, published on 26 October 2026. The official launch establishes the publication date. The preserved source summary records the supplied document; the research record explains the version comparison.

We prefer UK public statistics, legislation, regulators, Parliament and published research. A cited source may establish the current rule or explain a mechanism without validating Reform’s costing. Historical findings are dated; projections, modelled estimates and our economic inferences are identified as such. England-only and devolved policies are distinguished where relevant.

Figures attributed to the plan remain attributed unless corroborated. The official plan and linked policy page publish headline costings but not the model needed to reproduce the results. The major-line disclosure audit records what is provided and what is missing. Exact proposal-level fiscal outcomes therefore remain unverified. Sources were opened and reviewed for this edition; legislation and forecasts can change after the review date.
