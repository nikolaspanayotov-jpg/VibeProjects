# Reform UK Economic Rescue Plan — important points

Source: the attached `economic-rescue-plan.pdf` (36 pages), titled *Economic Rescue Plan*, with a foreword by Robert Jenrick MP.

These are source notes for the planned **rfrm-debunked** page. They record the document's proposals, arguments and claimed figures; they do not yet assess whether those claims are correct. Page references refer to the printed PDF page numbers.

**The document contains exactly 64 numbered measures in its “Growing the Pie” section.** They are reproduced in order below. Its tax, welfare and spending proposals are additional parts of the plan, summarised separately here.

## Headline argument and claims

- **Spend less, tax less and borrow less:** Reform argues that cuts to welfare, Net Zero programmes, foreign aid and public administration can fund tax reductions while reducing borrowing. It presents deregulation and increased energy production as ways to raise growth and lower inflation and interest rates. (pp. 4, 11, 23, 32)
- **Household tax relief:** the foreword promises households **£1,300 in tax relief**. The main personal tax commitments are a £15,000 personal allowance and £250 lower household energy bills. (pp. 4, 13–15)
- **Debt:** the document says public sector net debt passed **£3 trillion on 21 October**, equivalent to more than **£100,000 per household**. It says debt is forecast to remain around 95% of GDP in the short term. (p. 9)
- **Tax burden:** it says taxes have risen from **29.6% of GDP to a forecast 38.3%**, reaching their highest level since the aftermath of the Second World War. (p. 13)
- **Living standards:** it claims the average Briton is **30%, or £11,000, worse off** than if the pre-1997 growth trend had continued, measured using real household disposable income per person. (pp. 8, 23; endnotes 6 and 32)
- **Energy and investment:** it argues that Net Zero policies, planning restrictions and employment rules have made energy expensive, construction difficult and hiring risky. It claims its reforms would reverse these effects. (pp. 11, 23–28)

## Welfare and spending proposals

### Welfare

The plan refers to the separate *Making Welfare Work* paper and proposes the following. (pp. 18–19)

- Require work-capable long-term benefit claimants to work for their communities.
- Make **British citizenship a condition for almost every form of benefit**.
- Replace the disability-benefit system with one intended to keep claimants connected to employers, gradually removing unconditional cash support for mild cases.
- Protect people above State Pension Age and the cash awards of severely disabled people. Exempt existing “non-trivial, non-mental-health-related” claimants from reassessment under the new system for three years. Protect all children currently receiving child Disability Living Allowance, alongside protection for severely disabled children.
- Tighten fraud and error enforcement, change benefit indexation, and reintroduce the **two-child benefit cap**.
- The document claims non-pension welfare will cost the equivalent of **£7,000 per household annually by 2031**, and says one in seven Universal Credit claimants are neither British nor Irish citizens.
- Endnote 20 also describes a possible additional employer National Insurance cut in exchange for managing employee disabilities, with no obligations on firms employing fewer than six people. (p. 33)

### Net Zero spending

Cut taxpayer-funded Net Zero programmes, including industrial decarbonisation, transport grants, heat networks, land-based carbon schemes, the Boiler Upgrade Scheme, Carbon Capture and Storage, and the Electric Car Grant. End Great British Energy spending except for nuclear investments. Cut the additional costs attributed to decarbonising government buildings, fleets and operations. The document says these savings exclude bill levies, tax reliefs and regulatory mandates, which are addressed separately. (p. 19)

### Foreign aid

Cap the overseas budget at **£1 billion**. The document says this would retain funding for international organisations serving UK interests, aid to Ukraine and emergency humanitarian relief. (pp. 19–20)

### Civil service and public bodies

- Initially remove approximately **68,500 full-time-equivalent civil service roles**, or **13%**, while protecting frontline services. Halve policy roles, cut communications staff by 60%, and cut HR by around two-thirds. Claim **£4.2 billion in annual near-term cash savings**, plus **£1 billion annually in lower future pension liabilities**. Redundancy costs are said to be included. (pp. 20–21)
- Reduce the overall budgets of covered arm's-length bodies by **7.5%**, claiming approximately **£2.3 billion annual savings**. Exclude NHS England, Network Rail and bodies separately slated for abolition. Cuts would vary between bodies. Require periodic parliamentary approval for continued existence. (p. 21)
- Endnote 30 says welfare reassessments may require additional DWP staff, so the quoted civil service headcount reduction is a **gross reduction**. (p. 33)

### Universities and student loans

End taxpayer funding for the **bottom 20% of degree courses**, assessed by course-and-university combinations and long-term economic outcomes. Stop new student loans for these courses, while funding current students to finish. Allow exceptions for strategic or cultural value, explicitly protecting teacher, nursing, midwifery and allied medical training. Stop issuing student loans to foreign nationals and strengthen overseas loan collection. The plan counts an initial positive revaluation of the student-loan book from improved collection. (pp. 21–22)

### Additional spending and pension commitments

- Uncap medical school places and offer **0% student loans for nurses** to expand domestic NHS training. (p. 18)
- Fund the Defence Investment Plan shortfall and provide an additional spending allowance for defence, border security and public safety. The text says there is room for **defence spending of 3% by 2030**; the fiscal table's memo line describes **3% by 2029/30**. (pp. 18, 31)
- Preserve the **State Pension triple lock for the remainder of this Parliament and the next**. The document says its cost is already included in the OBR baseline used for the plan. (p. 32)
- The savings tables also count **ending migrant hotel spending**, starting in 2028/29. (pp. 22, 31)

## The 64 numbered rescue-plan measures

The numbers below match the PDF's original numbering. These are the proposals Reform labels “Over 60 Job Creating Measures”; the grouping follows its section headings. (pp. 23–29)

### Unlocking the North Sea — measures 1–5 (p. 24)

1. **Approve Rosebank immediately.** Approve the North Sea oil and gas project.
2. **Restart licensing for new oil and gas fields.** Resume issuing exploration licences.
3. **Legalise onshore exploration in new licences.** Allow onshore exploration through new licences.
4. **Abolish the Energy Profits Levy immediately.** Remove the additional tax on oil and gas profits.
5. **Change the North Sea regulator's objective and name.** Restore maximum economic extraction as its statutory objective, remove the Net Zero obligation, and rename the North Sea Transition Authority the Oil & Gas Authority.

### Reindustrialising Britain — measures 6–10 (p. 25)

6. **Abolish the Emissions Trading Scheme.** Remove the UK carbon-trading scheme.
7. **Stop implementing the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism.** Include the proposed charge affecting fertiliser imports.
8. **Repudiate AR7 contracts for difference and scrap AR8.** Reject the contracts from allocation round 7 and cancel allocation round 8 of the energy-support scheme.
9. **Implement the Fingleton Review in full immediately.** Adopt its recommendations for nuclear energy.
10. **Automatically approve French and South Korean nuclear designs as safe.** Legislate for recognition of these designs.

### Getting homes built in cities — measures 11–20 (pp. 25–26)

11. **Reduce affordable-housing requirements on brownfield development.** Set them at **0% outside major cities and 5% in major cities**.
12. **Introduce broad permitted development rights for brownfield housing.** Allow residential development under expanded permitted development rules.
13. **Repeal Biodiversity Net Gain rules.** Remove the biodiversity requirements for development.
14. **Leave the Aarhus Convention.** Withdraw from the environmental convention.
15. **Repeal and reform the Habitats Regulations.** Include the Nutrient Neutrality requirements.
16. **End what the document calls the ban on household boilers.** Remove the restriction described in the proposal.
17. **Permit air conditioning to meet overheating requirements.** Prohibit cooling hierarchy rules.
18. **Remove mandatory Energy Performance Certificate improvements.** Apply this to residential and commercial buildings.
19. **Revoke the Future Homes Standard.** Remove the proposed building standard.
20. **Repeal the Renters' Rights Act.** Reverse the legislation named in the document.

### Building critical national infrastructure — measures 21–31 (p. 26)

21. **Legislate approval for new runways.** Grant planning and environmental approval, including at Heathrow and Gatwick, with full overrides of other legislation.
22. **Auction airport take-off and landing slots.** Start with new and surrendered slots at Heathrow and Gatwick.
23. **Stop using “target-consistent” carbon pricing in project evaluation.** Remove that approach to valuing emissions.
24. **Exclude carbon emissions from individual planning decisions.** Prohibit considering them in individual applications.
25. **Classify all water reservoirs as Nationally Significant Infrastructure Projects.** Bring them into the national fast-track process.
26. **Make Development Consent Orders a single approval route.** Remove the requirement for agreement from the original authority.
27. **Restrict judicial review of planning decisions by the Secretary of State.** Limit court challenges to these decisions.
28. **Scrap Net Zero construction rules.** The example given is the requirement to build the Lower Thames Crossing without diesel.
29. **Scrap the social value test for procurement.** Remove this assessment from public purchasing.
30. **Remove launch-number limits at SaxaVord Spaceport.** Lift the cap on launches.
31. **Open completed sections of East-West Rail.** Resolve industrial disputes so already-built sections can operate.

### Joining the AI revolution — measures 32–33 (p. 26)

32. **Review regulatory barriers to AI adoption rapidly.** Examine obstacles across the economy.
33. **Explicitly permit on-site dispatchable electricity generation.** Allow gas engines or turbines and other controllable generation for industrial developments, including data centres.

### Reducing the cost of hiring British workers — measures 34–38 (p. 27)

34. **Cut employer National Insurance for British workers to 13.8%.** Reduce it from 15%.
35. **Introduce a migrant worker levy.** Aim to discourage hiring low-skilled foreign workers and encourage investment in British workers or automation.
36. **Repeal the Employment Rights Act.** Include the mandatory trade-union access provisions.
37. **Clarify workplace-rights duties concerning training.** State that taking “reasonable steps” does not require mandatory training courses.
38. **Reform the Construction Skills Certification Scheme (CSCS).** Allow experienced tradespeople to substitute experience for qualifications on government contracts and open the system to competition.

### Unleashing the City to invest in the real economy — measures 39–41 (p. 28)

39. **Review bank capital requirements.** Aim to encourage more lending to businesses and the wider economy.
40. **Exclude Net Zero considerations from financial regulators' remits.** Send a remit letter covering regulators including the Bank of England and Financial Conduct Authority.
41. **Repeal climate-related financial disclosure rules.** Remove these reporting requirements.

### Getting motorists moving — measures 42–48 (p. 28)

42. **Remove airport surface-access strategies aimed at reducing car traffic.** The stated aim is to reduce pressure on parking and drop-off charges.
43. **Abolish electric and hybrid vehicle mandates.** Remove the rules the document describes as banning new purely internal-combustion cars by 2030 and all non-zero-emission vehicles, including vans, by 2035.
44. **Apply domestic GB drivers' hours rules to all UK HGV trips.** Allow all journeys within the UK to use those rules.
45. **Prohibit Low-Traffic Neighbourhoods.** Ban these traffic-management schemes.
46. **Support construction of the M4 Relief Road.** Offer UK Treasury or British Sovereign Wealth Fund support to the Welsh Government.
47. **Delay a new car's first MOT until its fourth year.** Change the Great Britain requirement from three years to four.
48. **Abolish the Renewable Transport Fuel Obligation.** Remove the transport-fuel requirement.

### Using Brexit freedoms — measures 49–52 (p. 28)

49. **Replace the GDPR with a New Zealand-style Privacy Act.** Seek to retain data-protection adequacy.
50. **Fully re-legalise Imperial units.** Expand their permitted use as proposed in the document.
51. **Review all unchanged EU law.** Repeal or modify laws judged unsuitable for UK circumstances.
52. **Automatically repeal unchanged EU law after five years.** Retain individual laws only through a specific affirmative procedure.

### General deregulatory measures — measures 53–64 (pp. 28–29)

53. **Make quarterly reporting voluntary under Making Tax Digital.** Remove mandatory quarterly reporting.
54. **Reduce legal obligations on non-executive directors of SMEs.** Ease duties for directors of small and medium-sized enterprises.
55. **Double temporary land-use allowances from 28 to 56 days annually.** Cover events, markets and other activities without planning permission.
56. **Make pavement dining licences automatic and permanent unless revoked.** Replace automatic expiry after two years.
57. **Generally allow shops to stay open until 10pm.** Exclude Sundays from this proposal.
58. **Repeal board diversity and inclusion rules.** Remove these requirements.
59. **Remove the Sustainable Aviation Fuel mandate.** End the fuel requirement.
60. **Abolish Extended Producer Responsibility.** Remove the scheme the document calls the “packaging tax”.
61. **Remove preference for the BBC and Channel 4 on streaming platforms.** Include YouTube and other streaming sites.
62. **Remove the Public Sector Equality Duty.** Abolish the duty.
63. **Give all existing QUANGOs a five-year sunset.** Automatically abolish them unless their continued existence is justified.
64. **Require new regulations to expire within five years unless renewed.** Complete a post-implementation review before expiry and require ministers to actively recommit to the rules.

- The fiscal table also includes **increased immigration fees**, raising a stated £1.36 billion in 2027/28 and £3.52 billion in 2031/32. (p. 31)
- It projects public sector net borrowing of **£46.86 billion in 2029/30** and **£30.89 billion in 2030/31**, respectively 26% and 48% below its government comparison. (p. 31)
- It reports **£26.59 billion headroom against the deficit rule** and **£36.68 billion against the balance-sheet rule** in 2029/30. (p. 31)
- It shows the **current budget deficit increasing by £4.24 billion in 2027/28**, before improving in subsequent years, even though total net borrowing is shown falling in every year of the plan. (p. 31)
- Reform does **not commit to its own future fiscal rules** in this document. It tests the plan against the government's rules and says its own rules may differ. (p. 30)
- The document says policy timing and sequencing may change depending on conditions at election time, except for **“First 100 Days” pledges**. (p. 30)
