Growth is only half the question.

Can durable demand and better compute economics create value for each share at the price investors pay?

Three tailwinds

  1. Enterprise spending is expanding. More than 11,900 customers each generated $100,000+ of trailing-year revenue by September. Durable usage could deepen deployment. [1]
  2. More routes to customers. AWS, Google Cloud and Azure provide distribution. Hardware diversity could improve capacity choice and economics. [1]
  3. Efficiency can improve returns. Better utilisation and useful output per compute dollar could help growth convert to cash. This is an analytical opportunity, not an established outcome.

Three headwinds

  1. Costs can arrive before returns. Q3 compute expense grew faster than revenue. The filing expects $70.7bn of compute/infrastructure payments over the twelve months after September. [1]
  2. Demand can change quickly. Consumption-based activity supplied about 79% of nine-month revenue; the top five customers contributed about 25%. Competition and restrictions add risk. [1]
  3. Business growth can be diluted. Awards, note conversions and new financing affect economic shares. Limited shareholder control and an expensive purchase price can constrain returns. [1]

Read the measures together. Adjusted income excludes stock compensation and non-cash charity. Expected IPO-related RSU expense is approximately $14.2bn, distinct from cash tax withholding. Operating cash inflow excludes investment and financing. The commitment aggregate is now $900.9bn. Customer growth alone does not establish recurring margins or cash returns.

ANTHROPIC IPO / WHAT TO RESOLVE / 2 OF 2

Price the terms. Follow the evidence.

Price and share count
Proposed $70–$78 range for 825m public shares. At $74, offering-summary A+N shares imply $968.4bn; the expanded worksheet implies $1.333tn on a different basis. Final price pending; company timetable remains conditional. [1]
Proceeds and supply
Estimated net company proceeds: $84.6bn including the conditional $26bn placement, at $74 without the option; expected RSU tax use $3.8bn. Up to 316m existing shares may become eligible on day one; eligibility is not selling. [1]
Retail access
Schwab, Fidelity, Robinhood, Trade-PMR, SoFi and E*TRADE are named prospective platforms. Overall retail allocations are expected up to 20%, based on demand. Allocation can be smaller or zero. UK/EEA/Swiss buyers use the final global IPO price; FX/intermediary costs can add to it. The amendable $120 EEA/Swiss ceiling is not a UK surcharge. [1] [2]

Upcoming evidence, with its status

  • 18/19 November EST · expected pricing/trading. Company timetable, subject to clearance and conditions. Reported six-times book coverage is attributed demand, not an allocation or return guarantee. [5] [6]
  • December 2026 · expected SpaceXAI delivery. Management expects full capacity; monthly fees would reach about $3.1bn. Watch delivery and utilisation. Initial non-cancelable periods end in January/March 2027; cancellation then requires 90 days' notice. [1]
  • 9–10 November · product and business-demand evidence. New Fable/Mythos 5.2 models may improve task economics. [4] October run-rate is $81bn, annualised recent activity, not GAAP annual revenue. Final terms and future results remain key. [1]
SpaceX lesson: the entry price matters. At the same 29 October close of $143.89, historical returns were +6.6% from the $135 offer, −4.1% from the $150 first trade, and −31.9% from the selected $211.39 early high close. These selected hindsight comparisons exclude costs and taxes and do not forecast Anthropic. [3]
