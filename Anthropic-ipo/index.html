From revenue to operating profit

The important question is how much growth survives the cost of delivering it. Anthropic's second quarter turned an operating profit, while its first half remained loss-making.

A profitable quarter does not establish sustained profitability. Management expects margins to fluctuate as more compute comes online, with more expensive short-term capacity adding near-term pressure.

Understand the net loss, cash balance and margin definitions

The H1 2026 net loss was $80.748bn, including $72.388bn of fair-value losses on notes, derivatives and SAFEs, largely associated with rising company valuations. It was not an equivalent cash outflow. Conversion terms still affect the economic value of each share.

Cash and short-term investments were $131.651bn at 30 June 2026, excluding $2.529bn of restricted cash. These are dated balances, largely supported by capital raising. The $17.181bn investing cash outflow mainly reflected purchases of securities, not infrastructure capex alone.

Note 8's $2.380bn cost of revenue implies a calculated 85.4% margin against H1 revenue. That measure excludes substantial model-development compute in R&D and channel costs in sales and marketing. It is not a margin after all costs necessary to sustain the business.

What can build value. What can hold it back.

The same forces often work in both directions. These are the six areas that deserve the most attention.

TailwindsHeadwinds

Enterprise demand

Tailwind

More than 11,900 customers generated at least $100,000 of trailing-year revenue by September 2026, versus more than 950 a year earlier. Deeper deployment can turn Claude into part of everyday work.

Headwind

About 80% of H1 revenue was usage-based. Customer activity can fall quickly. The top five customers generated approximately 25% of revenue in the first nine months of 2026.

Watch: durable customer expansion, spending concentration and usage that persists beyond early adoption.

Customer adoptionConcentration risks
Revenue quality and the 900% retention figure

Anthropic estimates that 5%–7% of revenue in the first nine months of 2026 came from customers later identified as violating usage policies or commercial terms. Stronger enforcement can reduce future revenue.

Approximately 900% net revenue retention at September 2026 measures revenue expansion in a qualified customer cohort, comparing a three-month period with the equivalent period a year earlier. It excludes Claude Pro and Max subscribers. It is neither customer retention nor a reliable long-term growth assumption. The spending tiers overlap and use actual trailing-year revenue, not signed annual contract value.

Metric definitions

Model quality and useful products

Tailwind

Claude's models, platform, coding agent and knowledge-work applications offer several ways into a customer's workflow. Research and customer feedback can reinforce one another.

Headwind

OpenAI, Google and open-weight competitors can narrow the advantage or pressure prices. Leadership requires continued research spending and retention of scarce talent.

Watch: better model performance translating into paid, recurring work—not benchmark gains alone.

Products and competition

Compute efficiency and capacity

Tailwind

Training and inference improvements can produce more useful output per dollar. Anthropic supports Trainium, TPUs, NVIDIA GPUs and AMD GPUs, increasing hardware flexibility.

Headwind

Capacity is ordered ahead of demand. Power, chips or delivery delays can restrict growth; excess capacity can leave costs running ahead of revenue.

Watch: utilization, cost per useful task, capacity delivery and the premium paid for temporary supply.

Compute strategy and cost outlook

Distribution and partners

Tailwind

AWS, Google Cloud, Azure and integration partners help Claude reach businesses inside existing purchasing and security arrangements.

Headwind

Marketplace channels generated 48% of H1 revenue. Some partners also supply capacity, provide capital and compete with Anthropic, creating complex incentives and fees.

Watch: channel economics, independent funding and customer access if a major partnership changes.

Channel and partner risks

Trust, safety and market access

Tailwind

Reliable, secure systems can support adoption in sensitive industries. Safety research is part of management's enterprise proposition.

Headwind

Misuse, unreliable agents, copyright claims, cyber incidents and government restrictions can damage trust or delay releases and sales.

Watch: reliable deployment, the outcome of legal disputes, and permission to sell and release models.

The specific legal and political exposures

The $1.5bn Bartz copyright settlement, approved in July 2026, does not resolve all rights-holder and training-data cases. Other actions could require damages, licensing or changes to model development.

The filing describes distinct US government proceedings: a largely favorable district-court ruling in August and an adverse D.C. Circuit decision on the separate Section 4713 supply-chain designation in September, followed by a rehearing request. A favorable ruling in one proceeding did not end all restrictions.

It also reports worldwide restrictions on Fable 5 and Mythos 5 in June 2026 that were later lifted after safeguards changed. These are dated filing disclosures; subsequent developments must be checked separately.

Capital and ownership

Tailwind

Capital access and strategic financing can support new research and secure capacity when infrastructure is scarce.

Headwind

Conditional funding, equity compensation and public-benefit initiatives can dilute shares. Public investors have limited influence over company decisions.

Watch: the terms of future funding and cash generated per economic share.

Governance and control

The cost of staying ahead

Compute is committed ahead of the revenue it may support. Timing, flexibility and utilization matter as much as the headline amount.

  1. Commit to capacity
  2. Receive infrastructure
  3. Incur costs and payments
  4. Serve customer demand

The move toward Anthropic Direct—leasing chips and hosting them in dedicated facilities—may improve long-term economics. It also brings larger upfront requirements and equipment, power and construction risks.

MD&A, contractual obligationsNote 13 commitments

What reaches each share?

Control, economic ownership and available-for-sale shares are separate. Company growth can be offset by a larger share count or a lower valuation multiple.

Who controls the company?

Public Class A shares have one vote each. Class N shares have equivalent economic rights but generally no votes. The Long-Term Benefit Trust uses Class T to elect a majority of authorized directors; Founder LLC's Class F has 50.1% of voting power on matters submitted to shareholders generally, including election of the other directors.

The public-benefit structure can support long-term research and trust, while allowing decisions that do not maximize financial returns. These mechanisms limit public shareholder influence and takeover pressure. The LTBT does not run daily operations.

Governance structure
What can create economic dilution?

Compensation awards, convertible financing and public-benefit distributions can increase external economic shares. The initial 846.8m-share contribution to the wholly owned benefit fund represented 5% of the specified June fully diluted base; fund-held shares are not outstanding for accounting purposes. External transfers or sales require separate dilution treatment.

The board's discretionary framework permits up to an additional 2% of the defined base at successive 50% share-price milestones. Each requires the closing price to exceed the threshold on 90 of 120 trading days and the preceding ten-day average to exceed it. A one-day jump does not automatically trigger an issuance.

June illustrations included a $2.9bn cumulative RSU charge if the IPO had occurred then, plus $23.7bn of remaining RSU compensation over approximately 2.1 years. Final IPO charges were not specified. Financing terms also matter: Broadcom notes, if drawn, can carry paid-in-kind interest and discounted conversion prices that worsen under specified revenue conditions.

Public-benefit frameworkCompensation and financing risks
When can more shares become tradable?

Some employees and other holders may transfer up to 10% of their defined holdings from the first trading day and another 35% after FY2026 results. Other holders have phased releases starting 27, 48, 62 and 76 days after the prospectus. Founders and certain large holders have longer restrictions with interim releases.

These percentages are holder-specific, not percentages of the entire company. Final dates and quantities require final terms. An unlock permits a sale; it does not prove a sale occurred. Converting existing Class N into Class A changes voting/tradable shares without creating new economic ownership a second time.

What SpaceX's first months can teach us

A strong business can still be a difficult investment at the wrong price. SpaceX's early trading exposed the tension between growth, capital spending and share supply.

The listed SpaceX includes launch, Starlink, xAI/Grok, X and, since August, Cursor. Its AI exposure makes this a more relevant comparison than the company names suggest.

  • The entry price matters. An IPO allocation and an opening-market purchase are different valuations.
  • Growth needs funding. Rising revenue does not settle the question of future cash requirements.
  • Unlocks are not a forecast. Eligibility to sell is different from actual sales and investor expectations.

Capital models

Anthropic's historical cloud-service costs and future direct-equipment leases are not captured by its low cash property purchases alone. SpaceX's adjusted EBITDA excludes substantial depreciation and compensation. Use consistently defined operating and cash measures before applying valuation multiples.

Control and dilution

Musk was expected to control about 82.4% of SpaceX votes at the IPO before full option exercise. Anthropic combines LTBT board rights, Founder LLC voting power and public-benefit duties. Both limit public investor influence through different mechanisms. Acquisitions and compensation affect SpaceX's economic share count; Anthropic also has the benefit framework and partner-note conversions to consider.

What transfers between the cases

Valuation, financing and float matter to both. Launch and telecom execution risk is not the same as frontier-model competition. Holding both stocks would also expose an investor to shared AI demand and funding conditions, rather than two wholly independent themes.

What to know before pricing the shares

A great business can be an expensive investment. The missing offer terms are central to judging what an investor is being asked to pay.

  • Price and economic share countInclude Class N, awards and relevant note conversions. The listed Class A count alone is insufficient.
  • Where the proceeds goSeparate new company funding from selling-holder proceeds and RSU tax-settlement cash.
  • Cash against future obligationsUpdate cash, collateral, leases and the timing of minimum payments. Conditional funding is not cash in hand.
  • Demand against delivered capacityTrack utilization, useful output per compute dollar and the cost of short-term supply.
  • Complete recent financialsSeparate GAAP revenue from annualized activity, and normalized operations from accounting adjustments.
  • The actual release scheduleUse holder-specific dates and quantities. Do not assume all shares stay locked for 180 days.

Three ways the investment case could develop

Growth converts to cash

Frontier leadership persists, customers expand, and efficiency and utilization improve faster than the cash burden.

Evidence to seek: durable demand and cash generation after reinvestment.

Growth needs continued funding

Revenue grows strongly, but capacity arrives in costly steps and new financing or awards expand the share base.

Evidence to seek: attractive financing terms and improving returns on capital.

Demand or financing disappoints

Competition, restrictions or customer retrenchment reduce demand while committed costs and funding needs remain.

Evidence to seek: flexibility to cut costs and sufficient liquidity under stress.

The discipline: assess growth, normalized cash generation, valuation and dilution together. These scenarios are analytical conditions, not forecasts or assigned probabilities. This guide gives no share-price target while final pricing and capitalization remain incomplete in the source filing.

Plain-English definitions
Operating profit
Revenue after operating expenses, before financing, investment and tax effects. GAAP and adjusted figures can differ.
Adjusted EBITDA
A company-defined measure that excludes interest, taxes, depreciation, amortization and other adjustments. It is not free cash flow.
Float
Shares available for public trading. It is smaller than total economic shares when holdings are restricted.
Lock-up
A restriction on transfers for specified holders. Expiry permits selling; it does not require it.
Dilution
A reduction in an existing holder's economic participation when additional economic shares are issued.
Run-rate revenue
Anthropic annualizes 28-day consumption fees by multiplying by 13 and monthly subscriptions by 12. It is not a revenue forecast or contracted recurring revenue.
Net revenue retention
Expansion, contraction and churn in a qualified existing-customer revenue cohort. Anthropic excludes Pro and Max subscribers.
Contract ceiling
A maximum stated amount under specified terms, not necessarily an unconditional obligation or revenue already earned.
