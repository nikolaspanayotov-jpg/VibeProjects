# Budget 26: five design and content improvements

Reviewed against the implemented page, editorial markdown and Budget PDF. This is a source and content review; browser visual verification remains unavailable in this environment.

1. **Make expanded text add useful information.** Keep the announcement and principal figure visible. Use Details and sources for eligibility, exclusions, implementation stages and caveats. When there is no additional explanation, use Sources. The original page had ten exact summary/detail duplicates and 35 expanded paragraphs that began with the full summary, including those ten. **Applied:** none of the revised detail paragraphs repeats or starts with its full summary. Bus fares, Trading allowance and Corporation Tax now have source-only disclosures. For example, the energy-payment detail now explains the £100 million targeted-support allocation and devolved funding; the mileage detail explains the higher-mileage rate.

2. **Make visible summaries complete and consequential.** Lead with what changes for the reader, use complete sentences and avoid duplicating dates already in the metadata. **Applied:** corrected all 15 lowercase summary openings, clarified several short summaries and moved supporting figures into expanded details. Spending discipline now foregrounds the planned 20% Civil Service administration-budget reduction, compared with the previous 16% plan. The original editorial source is preserved.

3. **Separate location, eligibility and policy status.** Where should consistently name geographical coverage. It currently sometimes describes a taxpayer group, a rule or a consultation: private hire says “Turnover threshold under consultation,” while Making Tax Digital says “Income tax; some groups deferred.” **Proposed:** use separate Where, Who and Status fields where needed; check the source before assigning a geographic scope. Keep consultation qualifications visible.

4. **Help readers navigate the long policy list.** Mobile search and topic controls scroll out of view while readers work through 48 entries. **Proposed:** add a small Back to filters link after each topic and mark the current main section in the sticky navigation. Keep the mobile header compact and preserve keyboard focus and anchor clearance. The existing navigation CSS already supports an active-section state.

5. **Give the OBR section a short forecast overview.** The chart requires readers to choose metrics and find the significant turning points; its generated description currently focuses on the first and final years. **Proposed:** introduce the chart with three sourced figures: 2027 GDP growth of 1.0%, 2027 CPI inflation of 3.4%, and debt peaking at 97.3% of GDP in 2028–29. Explain how these support the OBR’s limited-headroom assessment. Update chart descriptions to mention intervening troughs or peaks, while retaining the accessible tables and distinction between forecasts and policy.

The copy revision leaves the layout, policy IDs, filters, forecasts and source documents intact. Content inventory, original numerical values, citations, syntax and modelled DOM interactions have been checked; these do not establish native browser behaviour or visual quality.
