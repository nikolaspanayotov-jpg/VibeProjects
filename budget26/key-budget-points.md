# Budget 2026 — key points

**Budget date: 28 October 2026. Prepared: 28 October 2026.**

Based on the attached HM Treasury Budget, HMRC’s tax legislation overview (OOTLAR), and the OBR’s Economic and fiscal outlook published today. Amounts are pounds sterling. Dates below are the announced commencement dates; measures requiring legislation or consultation are identified. England-only measures are labelled.

## Main takeaways

- **Immediate energy support:** a £100 payment for households in council tax bands A–D in England, and the temporary zero VAT rate on domestic electricity extended through June 2027.
- **Fuel and travel help:** the 5p fuel duty cut continues through June 2027; a £2 single bus fare cap returns in England outside London for 2027; higher business mileage rates become permanent.
- **Targeted tax increases:** savings and property income rates rise to **24%, 44% and 49%** from April 2027; banks face a temporary higher surcharge. The cash ISA limit stays at **£20,000**.
- **Business support:** higher trading allowance, additional business rates relief, a new Founders’ Relief, and expanded employee share options. The BADR lifetime limit is halved and the annual writing-down allowance is reduced.
- **Housing and pensions:** a £4.7 billion first-time buyer equity-loan scheme, another £1.5 billion for social and affordable housing, and a major change to State Pension uprating from April 2030.
- **The fiscal outlook remains tight:** the OBR forecasts slower growth in 2027, higher inflation, and little improvement in household purchasing power. Fiscal rules are met in its central forecast with limited room for adverse shocks.

The policy details below come principally from the [Budget document][budget], especially Chapters 3–6. The independent assessment is set out separately under [What the OBR says](#7-what-the-obr-says).

## 1. Households, energy and transport

| Measure | Key detail | Timing and coverage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Household Energy Payment | **£100** for households in council tax bands A–D; a £2.6 billion package, including devolved funding and £100 million of targeted local support. | Payments through local authorities from **January 2027** in **England**. Devolved governments receive funding through the Barnett formula. |
| Domestic electricity VAT | Temporary **0% VAT** extended, rather than reverting to 5% in April. Treasury estimates an approximately **£50 reduction in the annualised April 2027 price cap**; actual savings depend on consumption and prices. | Through **30 June 2027**. |
| Fuel duty | The **5p-per-litre cut** and freeze continue; the additional **3.7p red diesel duty cut** is extended too. | Through **30 June 2027**. The scheduled reversal and RPI uprating move to **1 July 2027**. |
| Bus fares | **£2 cap on single fares** for one year. | **1 January–31 December 2027**, **England outside London**. |
| Own-vehicle business mileage | **55p a mile** for the first 10,000 car/van business miles becomes permanent, effective from **6 April 2026**. The rate above 10,000 miles rises from **25p to 35p**. | Higher-mileage increase from **6 April 2027**. These are tax-free reimbursement/deduction rates. |
| Public EV charging | **£100 million over two years** to reduce costs at local, lower-powered public chargers. | From **spring 2027**, **Great Britain**. A taskforce will examine access to domestic tariffs at local chargers. |
| Food and heating oil | Tariffs suspended on **101 additional food products** and kerosene heating oil; fertiliser tariffs also suspended. | From **26 November 2026** to **31 December 2028** for food/heating oil, and **31 December 2027** for fertiliser. |

**Longer-term energy changes:** the government aims to abolish domestic gas standing charges in Great Britain by the end of 2027 or as soon as possible afterwards, with mitigations to distribute costs fairly. Electricity standing-charge reform will follow. It will introduce an energy-debt strategy and targeted debt relief, abandon the planned Gas Shipper Obligation levy, and consult on wholesale Contracts for Difference. A new energy cost-control framework is due at the 2027 spending review. These are reforms with further delivery work, rather than immediate bill reductions.

Sources: [Budget, pp. 41–49, 99–100 and 111–112][budget]; paragraphs 3.12–3.20 and 6.24–6.31.

## 2. Personal tax, savings and pensions

| Measure | Key point | Effective date |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Headline taxes | No increase announced to the headline rates of income tax on earnings, National Insurance or VAT. Specific income categories and duties change as set out below. | Budget 2026. |
| Savings income | Basic/higher/additional rates become **24% / 44% / 49%**, applying across the UK. | **6 April 2027**. |
| Property income | Basic/higher/additional rates become **24% / 44% / 49%** in **England, Wales and Northern Ireland**, subject to devolved rate-setting powers. | **6 April 2027**. |
| Cash ISA | Annual cash ISA limit retained at **£20,000**, reversing the planned reduction. An incentive for first-time stocks and shares ISA investors will be explored; its design is pending. | Retained from **April 2027**. |
| Trading allowance | Tax-free trading/miscellaneous income allowance rises from **£1,000 to £3,000** a year. | **6 April 2027**. |
| Pension Tax Allowance | Previously announced protection: automatic tax reduction for eligible people over State Pension age whose total income is no more than the full new State Pension, with a taper just above that level. | **6 April 2027–5 April 2030**; arrangements beyond that period remain to be decided. |
| Pension death benefits | Removes taxation arising solely because otherwise tax-free pension/death benefits are paid after the two-year deadline. Other tax conditions still apply. | Payments from **28 October 2026**. |

The savings/property increases are **a further two percentage points** on top of the two-point increases already announced in Budget 2025 for April 2027. This explains the resulting 24%/44%/49% rates. Existing savings allowances and ISA exemptions continue to matter.

**State Pension Triple Lock:** the existing highest-of-earnings, inflation or 2.5% formula continues through **April 2029**. From **April 2030**, the annual increase becomes the highest of inflation, 2.5%, or the increase needed to preserve the pension’s April 2029 value relative to earnings. This is intended to remove the repeated upward ratchet relative to earnings. The government links future savings to a National Care Service providing older people in **England** with personal care free at the point of use. Detailed care arrangements remain to be developed; the Casey commission will report in 2027. Treasury cites estimated pension savings of £15 billion a year by the late 2030s and £50 billion by the late 2040s, beyond the Budget forecast period.

Sources: [Budget, pp. 38–40, 93–95, 129 and 135–137][budget]; paragraphs 2.50–2.54, 5.23–5.25, 6.182 and 6.243–6.261. [OOTLAR, sections 1.1–1.5 and 1.18][ootlar] confirms the tax rates and start dates; it specifies a Pension Tax Allowance withdrawal rate of £1 for every £3 of income above the full new State Pension.

## 3. Work, benefits and housing

- **Minimum wages from 1 April 2027:** all rise by 3.9%. The National Living Wage for those aged 21 and over becomes **£13.21/hour**, the 18–20 rate **£11.27**, and the 16–17/eligible apprentice rate **£8.31**. Treasury estimates around 2.3 million workers will benefit.
- **Parental pay:** eligible self-employed fathers, partners and adopters in **Great Britain** gain access from **April 2029**; the government expects more than 150,000 parents a year to benefit.
- **Carer’s Allowance:** an earnings taper will replace losing the whole benefit after exceeding the weekly earnings limit. Implementation is planned during **2030–31**, benefiting around 48,000 carers in **England and Wales**.
- **Youth employment:** £100 million over two years expands local apprenticeship services. The Budget also confirms funding for the previously announced Jobs Guarantee and youth hiring support.
- **Your First Home, England:** a **£4.7 billion equity-loan scheme**, supporting about **70,000 first-time buyers**, with loans of up to **20%**, a minimum **2.5% deposit** and an initial **five-year interest-free period**. Household income caps are £110,000 outside London and £140,000 in London, with local property price caps. Pre-registration is planned by December 2026, applications from February 2027 and first loans from **April 2027**. Funding is reprioritised from existing departmental financial transactions.
- **Social and affordable housing, England:** an extra **£1.5 billion** for around **10,000 homes**, prioritising Social Rent and council homes; total programme investment exceeds £40 billion over ten years.
- **Lifetime ISA:** from **29 October 2026**, the withdrawal charge for qualifying first-home purchases **above £450,000** falls from **25% to 20%**. Other unauthorised withdrawals retain the 25% charge.
- **New First Time Buyer ISA:** replaces the Lifetime ISA for **new savers from April 2029**, with up to £4,000 annual contributions, a 25% bonus paid at qualifying withdrawal, a **£20,000 maximum bonus**, a £450,000 property cap and no upper age restriction. Existing Lifetime ISA holders can continue under current account rules.

Sources: [Budget, pp. 48–49, 89–94, 120–122 and 135][budget]; paragraphs 3.18–3.20, 5.5–5.9, 6.113–6.114, 6.125–6.128 and 6.239–6.240.

## 4. Businesses, founders and investment

| Measure | Key detail | Timing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Corporation Tax | Headline **25%** rate cap maintained under the Corporate Tax Roadmap. | Continuing commitment. |
| Business rates, England | Relief for qualifying pubs, social clubs and live music venues rises from **15% to 35%**. Venues with rateable value of £1 million or more do not receive the additional relief. | **1 April 2027**. |
| Small Business Rate Relief, England | For qualifying retail, hospitality and leisure properties, 100% relief threshold rises from **£12,000 to £18,000 rateable value**, with tapered relief up to **£21,000**. | **1 April 2027**. |
| Other RHL business rates, England | Qualifying bill increases capped at CPI inflation, subject to stated exceptions. | **2027–28 and 2028–29**. |
| Industrial electricity | British Industrial Competitiveness Scheme gives around **10,000 eligible manufacturing firms** an estimated **20–25% electricity discount**, including an additional payment reflecting earlier support. | **1 April 2027**, Great Britain; funding for a comparable Northern Ireland scheme. |
| Founders’ Relief | Proposed **10% CGT rate on up to £10 million of lifetime gains** for qualifying founders of innovative, high-growth companies. Detailed eligibility is subject to technical consultation and legislation. | Qualifying disposals from **6 April 2027**. |
| Business Asset Disposal Relief | Lifetime qualifying-gains limit falls from **£1 million to £500,000**; the relief rate is 18%. | Disposals from **6 April 2027**. |
| Employee share options | CSOP, to be known as EMI2, individual limit doubles to **£120,000**; exercise period extends to **15 years**. EMI’s post-disqualification exercise window increases from 90 to **180 days**. | **6 April 2027**. |
| Capital allowances | Main-rate first-year allowance rises from **40% to 50%**; main writing-down allowance falls from **14% to 12%**. Full expensing is maintained. | First-year change **1 January 2027**; writing-down change **1 April 2027** for Corporation Tax / **6 April 2027** for income tax. |
| R&D relief administration | Claim Notification Form removed; Additional Information Form simplified. | **1 April 2027** and **autumn 2027**, respectively. |
| Investment finance | National Wealth Fund receives **£15.4 billion additional capital**, including £2 billion for an Energy Wealth Fund. British Business Bank financial capacity increases by **£5 billion**. | Multi-year commitments; capacity, lending and investment are distinct from annual grant spending. |

Sources: [Budget, pp. 38–40, 117, 130 and 136–137][budget]; paragraphs 2.53–2.55, 6.82–6.83, 6.189–6.191 and 6.251–6.261.

## 5. How it is funded and changes to tax administration

- **Banks:** Corporation Tax surcharge temporarily rises from **3% to 6%**, **1 January 2027–31 March 2028**, then returns to 3%. This helps fund household support.
- **Gaming machines:** duty rises from **20% to 25%** for machines with stakes over £1 or prizes over £100 from **1 April 2027**. Supplier liability under profit-sharing arrangements is planned from April 2028.
- **Online marketplaces:** from **October 2027**, platforms collect VAT on sales by UK VAT-registered businesses and other UK businesses meeting a **£90,000 annual sales threshold per platform**. Personal sales, second-hand goods and business-to-business transactions are excluded.
- **Large private-hire operators:** liable for 20% VAT on the full fare from **2 January 2028**. The turnover threshold is under consultation, with **£50 million** the lead option.
- **HMRC modernisation:** mandatory VAT e-invoicing, more regular in-year Self Assessment payments for people making payments on account, and VAT/PAYE direct-debit arrangements are planned from **April 2029**. Direct debit collects outstanding balances after the payment cut-off; other payment methods remain available beforehand.
- **Making Tax Digital:** people joining income-tax MTD in April 2027 receive relief from late-submission penalty points for their first four quarterly updates. Some taxpayer groups’ entry is deferred.
- **Other duties:** alcohol duty rises with RPI from **1 February 2027**, alongside more generous Small Producer Relief; car/van/motorcycle VED rises with RPI from April 2027; Air Passenger Duty is uprated from April 2028.

Sources: [Budget, pp. 123–124, 130–137][budget]; paragraphs 6.145–6.154, 6.201, 6.219, 6.225–6.231 and 6.252.

**Additional points in OOTLAR:** most unused pension funds/death benefits entering inheritance tax from **April 2027** and mileage-based EV duty from **April 2028** are previously announced measures being implemented. EV rates are 3p/mile for battery/hydrogen vehicles and 1.5p for plug-in hybrids. OOTLAR also sets out April 2027 cryptoasset reforms and a permanent 35% levy on exceptional oil/gas revenues after the Energy Profits Levy ends. Its Table 1 identifies measures absent from the main Budget report. [OOTLAR, sections 1.27, 1.35, 1.45, 1.48 and 1.76][ootlar].

## 6. Public spending, devolution and strategic priorities

- **Spending discipline:** the next spending review is in **2027**. The government says day-to-day departmental spending grows overall in real terms, but individual departments may face real cuts. Civil Service administration budgets are to fall **20% by 2029–30**, compared with the previous 16% plan.
- **Defence funding:** the remaining **£4.7 billion** needed for the announced Defence Investment Plan is funded through **£3 billion of further departmental capital-budget reductions** and **£1.7 billion from the capital Reserve**. The 2027 spending review will detail the path to **3% of GDP in 2030** and **3.5% in 2035**.
- **Ukraine:** over **£10 billion of additional financial support**, including lending/export-finance capacity and **£500 million emergency military support**; this is separate from the continuing £3 billion annual military support commitment.
- **Fiscal devolution, England:** mayors gain a share of business rates from **2027–28** and income tax from **2028–29**. Good Growth Funds receive **£285 million transitional seed funding** in 2027–28 and a **£1.5 billion borrowing facility** from April 2028. Strategic authorities will be enabled to introduce visitor levies.
- **AI and security:** an additional **£120 million** for the AI Security Institute, **£165 million** for AI biosecurity, **£40 million** for a National Agentic Security Unit and **£140 million** of additional Sovereign AI investment/procurement activity. Further cyber measures and public-service AI adoption are also funded.

The often-quoted **£40 billion extra annual day-to-day spending by 2028–29** and **£120 billion additional investment over the Parliament** compare total plans with the previous government’s spring 2024 plans. They are broader comparisons across the Parliament, rather than new money announced solely today.

Sources: [Budget, pp. 31–35, 113–114, 119–120 and 122–123][budget]; paragraphs 2.2–2.7, 2.14, 6.40–6.44, 6.99–6.108 and 6.134–6.144.

### Independent assessment

- **Living standards:** real disposable income per person in 2030–31 is about 2% above pre-pandemic levels and ¾% below March’s forecast.
- **Fiscal rules:** **£15 billion** current-budget headroom and **£24 billion** financial-liabilities headroom in 2029–30; **56% probability** of meeting the current-budget rule, down from 59%.
- **Debt:** falls after its peak but ends **no lower than now**, qualifying the Budget’s executive summary.
- **Tax:** the tax-to-GDP ratio exceeds its previous record in 2027–28; frozen thresholds contribute.
- **Renters:** higher property taxes risk reducing rental supply and raising long-term rents.
- **Pensions:** uprating reform improves long-term sustainability; care reform’s cost remains unsettled.
- **Risks:** a persistent energy shock could add **£17 billion** to 2027–28 borrowing; a one-point interest-rate shift changes medium-term borrowing by about **£16 billion**.
- **Growth:** no material real-GDP forecast adjustment attributed to the fiscal changes.

Source: [OBR, paragraphs 1.2–1.4, 1.22–1.29, 2.19, 3.41 and 3.46][obr-pdf]. Forecasts and scenarios carry uncertainty.
