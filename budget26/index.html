The Budget at a glance

Six things to take away

  • Bills

    Some breathing space for energy costs

    £100 energy payments in England and the electricity VAT extension lead the immediate support.

  • Travel

    Fuel relief lasts through June

    The 5p duty cut continues; a £2 bus cap returns outside London and business mileage rates increase.

  • Savings

    Higher rates, the same cash ISA limit

    Savings and property income rates rise to 24%, 44% and 49%; the cash ISA limit remains £20,000.

  • Business

    Support comes with targeted changes

    More rates relief and new founder incentives sit alongside a lower Business Asset Disposal Relief limit and writing-down allowance.

  • Housing & pensions

    Changes with different time horizons

    First-home equity loans start in April 2027; State Pension uprating changes from April 2030.

  • The outlook

    The public finances remain tight

    The OBR forecasts weaker growth in 2027 and limited room to absorb further shocks.

Bills and travel

Immediate support, temporary relief and the longer-term energy changes.

Household Energy Payment

A £100 payment for households in council tax bands A–D in England, delivered through local authorities. The £2.6 billion package also includes devolved funding and targeted local support.

When
From January 2027
Where
England; devolved funding separately
Details and sources for Household Energy Payment

Of the wider package, £100 million is earmarked for targeted local support. Devolved governments receive funding through the Barnett formula, rather than the England payment being a UK-wide entitlement.

HM Treasury Budget, printed p. 111, 6.24.

Domestic electricity VAT

The temporary 0% VAT rate on domestic electricity is extended through June 2027. Treasury estimates around £50 off the annualised April price cap; actual savings depend on use and prices.

When
Through 30 June 2027
Where
UK extension from April 2027
Details and sources for Domestic electricity VAT

Without this extension, VAT would have returned to 5% in April 2027. The estimated saving above is an annualised price-cap comparison, not a fixed payment to each household.

HM Treasury Budget, printed p. 132, 6.210.

Fuel duty

The 5p-per-litre cut and freeze continue; the additional 3.7p red diesel duty cut is extended too.

When
Relief through 30 June 2027
Where
UK
Details and sources for Fuel duty

The scheduled reversal of the temporary cut and uprating in line with the Retail Prices Index (RPI) move to 1 July 2027.

HM Treasury Budget, printed p. 132, 6.211–6.212.

Longer-term energy reforms

The government aims to remove gas standing charges, tackle energy debt and reform how electricity costs reach bills. Delivery and detailed design still lie ahead.

When
2027 onward; delivery work remains
Where
Great Britain for standing-charge reforms

Further design and delivery

Details and sources for Longer-term energy reforms

The target for removing domestic gas standing charges is the end of 2027, or as soon as possible afterwards, with mitigations to distribute costs fairly. Electricity standing-charge reform follows. The programme also includes an energy-debt strategy and targeted relief, dropping the planned Gas Shipper Obligation levy, and consultation on wholesale Contracts for Difference. An energy cost-control framework is due at the 2027 spending review.

HM Treasury Budget, printed p. 111, 6.25–6.28.

Tax, savings and pensions

Rates, allowances and the changes to retirement income.

Headline tax rates

No increase announced to the headline rates of income tax on earnings, National Insurance or VAT.

When
Budget of 28 October 2026
Where
UK headline commitments; devolved powers apply
Details and sources for Headline tax rates

The commitment concerns headline rates. Specific categories of income and duties still change: the separate savings-income and property-income measures set out the new rates for those categories.

Tax on savings income

Basic, higher and additional rates on savings income become 24%, 44% and 49% from April 2027. Existing savings allowances and ISA exemptions continue to matter.

When
6 April 2027
Where
UK
Details and sources for Tax on savings income

This is a further two-percentage-point increase on top of the two-point rise already announced in Budget 2025 for April 2027. The combined changes explain the resulting rates.

HM Treasury Budget, printed p. 136, 6.247.

6 April 2027
Where
England, Wales and Northern Ireland; devolved powers apply
Details and sources for Tax on property income

As with savings income, this adds two percentage points to the two-point increase announced in Budget 2025. The commitment to unchanged headline tax rates on earnings is separate from this property-income measure.

Pension Tax Allowance

Previously announced protection reduces small tax bills for eligible pensioners whose total income is no more than the full new State Pension. A taper applies just above that level.

When
6 April 2027–5 April 2030
Where
Eligible people over State Pension age

Earlier policy confirmed

Details and sources for Pension Tax Allowance

The reduction is automatic for eligible pensioners and is withdrawn at £1 for every £3 of income above the full new State Pension. Arrangements after 5 April 2030 remain to be decided (OOTLAR section 1.4).

HM Treasury Budget, printed p. 129, 6.182.

HMRC tax overview (OOTLAR), section 1.4.

Pension death benefits

Removes taxation arising solely because otherwise tax-free pension/death benefits are paid after the two-year deadline.

When
Payments from 28 October 2026
Where
UK pension tax rules
Details and sources for Pension death benefits

Other tax conditions still apply. The change removes the tax charge caused solely by missing the two-year payment deadline; it does not make all pension death-benefit payments tax-free.

HM Treasury Budget, printed p. 135, 6.243.

State Pension Triple Lock

The current Triple Lock continues through April 2029. From April 2030, an adjusted formula aims to preserve its April 2029 value relative to earnings and remove the repeated upward ratchet.

When
Adjusted formula from April 2030
Where
State Pension; proposed care service in England
Details and sources for State Pension Triple Lock

Through April 2029, uprating remains the highest of earnings growth, inflation or 2.5%. From April 2030, it becomes the highest of inflation, 2.5%, or the increase needed to maintain the pension’s April 2029 value relative to earnings. The government links future savings to a National Care Service offering older people in England personal care free at the point of use. Detailed care arrangements are still to be developed, with the Casey commission reporting in 2027. Treasury estimates pension savings of £15 billion a year by the late 2030s and £50 billion by the late 2040s. Those estimates sit beyond the Budget forecast period.

HM Treasury Budget, printed p. 93, 5.23–5.25.

Why 24%, 44% and 49%?

The increases are a further two percentage points on top of the two-point increases announced in Budget 2025 for April 2027. Existing savings allowances and ISA exemptions still matter.

HMRC tax overview (OOTLAR), sections 1.1–1.5 and 1.18.

Your First Home

A £4.7 billion equity-loan scheme aims to support around 70,000 first-time buyers in England, with up to 20% equity loans, a minimum 2.5% deposit and five initial interest-free years.

England

The assessment behind the numbers

  • Living standards

    real disposable income per person in 2030–31 is about 2% above pre-pandemic levels and ¾% below March’s forecast.

  • Fiscal rules

    £15 billion current-budget headroom and £24 billion financial-liabilities headroom in 2029–30; 56% probability of meeting the current-budget rule, down from 59%.

  • Debt

    falls after its peak but ends no lower than now, qualifying the Budget’s executive summary.

  • Tax

    the tax-to-GDP ratio exceeds its previous record in 2027–28; frozen thresholds contribute.

  • Renters

    higher property taxes risk reducing rental supply and raising long-term rents.

  • Pensions

    uprating reform improves long-term sustainability; care reform’s cost remains unsettled.

  • Risks

    a persistent energy shock could add £17 billion to 2027–28 borrowing; a one-point interest-rate shift changes medium-term borrowing by about £16 billion.

  • Growth

    no material real-GDP forecast adjustment attributed to the fiscal changes.

OBR report, paragraphs 1.2–1.4, 1.22–1.29, 2.19, 3.41 and 3.46. Forecasts and scenarios carry uncertainty.

About this edition

This guide covers the announcements of 28 October 2026. Announced measures, earlier policies being implemented, consultations and forecasts are distinguished where the source specifies them. A stated start date does not by itself mean legislation has passed.

Geographical scope and spending periods matter. England-only measures are labelled; annualised estimates, multi-year funding and lending capacity are kept distinct.

The supplied Google share links could not be opened, so the corresponding official GOV.UK publications were located and reviewed directly. This guide prioritises material changes rather than every technical amendment.
